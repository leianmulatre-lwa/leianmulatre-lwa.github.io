Free information still needs context

L’wazi is BIG LWA’s search and assistance interface. It can help surface relevant books, research and source links, but summaries and model answers may be incomplete or wrong. Follow the cited page, check publication date and original author, and distinguish a primary document from commentary about it.

Research often benefits from the perspectives that dominant catalogs neglect. BIG LWA is designed to make room for work by women, researchers of color, and community-led organizations, while pairing those perspectives with appropriate official sources when factual eligibility, law or safety is at stake.

Try a source-first research workflow

  1. Ask a specific question

    Search L’wazi or a public encyclopedia to identify the relevant subject.

  2. Read the cited source

    Check names, dates, organizations, authorship and links rather than stopping at an AI summary.

  3. Save your trail

    Use the Library, Journal or Projects to keep the materials you can revisit.

Free does not mean every book is downloadable.

Access, borrowing terms, editions and licensing vary. BIG LWA doesn't claim that third-party publishers offer unrestricted copies, nor that a search answer replaces a primary source.

Questions people ask

Does BIG LWA provide a free encyclopedia?

BIG LWA connects to free, public encyclopedic and research sources. It does not host a complete proprietary encyclopedia or guarantee the accuracy of a search summary.

Where can I find free books?

Open Library and Project Gutenberg are helpful starting points, along with source-linked books surfaced through BIG LWA Library.

Can I trust L’wazi's answers as citations?

Use L’wazi to find sources, then cite the original publication or record. The model answer alone is not a primary source.
